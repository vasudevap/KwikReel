01

High-level: two planes, one boundary

The pipeline that touches your footage stays entirely on your Mac. Atlas never receives media — it receives a proposed action and the evidence behind it (rationale, counts, the timeline summary), shows it to you, records your decision, and lets the local run continue. That split is the only way a cloud-capable control plane can coexist with the "original media never leaves the device" constraint.

The reconciliation this diagram assumes — itself not yet decided

Atlas is a governed, potentially cloud-hosted control plane; this project's hard rule is that original media never leaves the device. The only way both hold is the split above: Atlas orchestrates, approves, and audits using rationale and metadata, while media, analysis, and render stay local. That division is a sensible projection — not an ADR, and not something either repo has specified.

02

Two surfaces, two jobs

You'd interact with the system in two different places. The editorial work happens at the agent surface; the governance work happens in Atlas. Today only the left card is in the plan — and even that is a command line, not a GUI (ADR-001: "no polished review UI in the POC").

The agent surface in POC scope

local CLI · plain-language report · draft.mp4
zsh — ~/footage/hawaii-2026
$ kwikreel generate ./clips --track song.mp3 --keep 3,8,14 --dur 60 report.txt kept clip_014 · audio: laughter peak 0.82 · steady kept clip_031 · user-declared must-include cut clip_022 · near-duplicate of 014 → draft.mp4 · 0:58 · 1080×1920
What you see
  • The rendered draft and a plain-language reason for every clip kept, cut, or timed the way it was
  • Source-to-subclip provenance in the JSON manifests
What you do
  • Mark must-include clips, set track / style / duration
  • Regenerate after changing a lock — iterate on the edit
  • Request the export (which then goes to Atlas to approve)

  • The immutable audit trail of every decision
What you do
  • Approve, reject, or ask for clarification — one human reviewer (Atlas's single-reviewer rule), with a recorded reason
  • Let policy deny what may never proceed — and honor withdrawable consent through audit + retention
